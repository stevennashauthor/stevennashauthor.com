The Falling Moon: a short guide to the films

The Moon is falling. Humanity is building ships, testing a way to change the Moon’s course, and arguing over who gets a chance to survive.

These short films introduce selected moments from The Falling Moon, the science-fiction novel by Steven Nash. They are glimpses of its world, not a complete adaptation. This guide stays with events already shown in the public films.

Choose how to watch

For one continuous introduction, open The Falling Moon — Start Here. The original book trailer is an optional quick overview; you do not need to watch it first.

  1. The Day the Moon Began to Fall

    Start with the catastrophe and its aftermath. The asteroid impact and the later flooding are separated by years. The novel begins in the world those events have left behind.

  2. A Home You Will Never Reach

    See what escape asks of the people who board. Pioneer is still being built, and its planned journey reaches beyond a single lifetime. Watch the contrast between the size of the ship and an empty berth.

  3. They Moved the Moon

    Follow a lunar-engineering test. A small success brings the scale of the remaining work into focus. The test does not reveal the novel’s final outcome.

  4. Her Application to Survive Was Denied

    Return to the people making the choices. Elena’s work and her adult daughter Isabella’s challenge bring the crisis into one room. The scene asks a question about responsibility; it does not settle it for you.

A few names to keep in view

Elena Vasquez selects people for the generation ships. Her daughter Isabella works in station operations.

Kwame Osei is working on the lunar-engineering response.

Pioneer is a generation ship under construction. Its long journey is a promise to later generations, not an arrival shown in these films.

Meridian Station and the shipyard are part of the evacuation effort. Grimaldi is the lunar setting of the engineering test.

From films to the novel

The films use selected passages and edited scenes. A shortened exchange is not an unabridged transcript, and a character’s argument is not necessarily the story’s final judgment. AI-generated imagery and voices depict fictional events.

The novel follows five people through evacuation, lunar engineering, faith, political fear and disputed records. You can enjoy the films on their own. If you want to try the book, open its Kindle page and choose Read sample. A paperback edition is also available.
